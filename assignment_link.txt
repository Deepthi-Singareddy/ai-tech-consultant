Why this project 
Software that cost a client $2,000 two years ago now sells for about $200, so a tech company must do 10 to 20 times more work to pay the same salaries. You will build the AI Tech Consultant that makes this possible. 
Think of a small family business that needs a website change. Two years ago they hired a developer for $2,000. Today the owner can describe the change to Claude or Cursor and get most of it done and in next 6months its expected to be 100% finish-line-delivery. 
Business owners will still hire developers, but they will not pay more than about $200, the highest plan of cursor / claude. That is a 90% drop in price. 
Code is now the fast part. The slow part is everything around it: knowing the product inside out, answering every customer email quickly and correctly, and turning long client meetings into a clear scope of work. A good tech consultant does all of this today, by hand. No company can grow 10 to 20 times on that. 
Your job is to build an AI Tech Consultant: one system that knows the product, handles the inbox, and turns meetings into scopes of work. It must get better every week from the people who use it. You are not building it for one company. Build it so any software or service business could plug in its own help center, inbox and meetings.

Task 1 — The Knowledge Brain 
Build the memory your users ask about anything: how the product works, how its settings behave, how refunds and cancellations are handled, and when the business goes out of its way to help a client. It must stay correct as the help center changes every day. 
Language: Python 
Every software business has a help center, and most customer questions are already answered somewhere inside it. The trouble is that nobody can find the answer fast, and the help center keeps changing. For this task, everyone uses the same public help center, so we can test every student on the same questions: the Brilliant Directories Solution Center. It is a real product with hundreds of articles. 
The help center has three levels. A category (also called a section) is a big area such as payments or design. Each category holds folders, and each folder is one topic. Each folder holds articles. Many articles include screenshots, and those images often carry the answer. The full list of pages lives in the sitemap: 
support.brilliantdirectories.com/support/sitemap.xml. 
What your Knowledge Brain must do: 
• Read everything once, politely. The first full read must not overload the help center's server. Decide how you batch it, how fast you go, and what happens if it stops halfway. 
• Understand the images. A screenshot is part of the answer. Your memory must know what each image shows and which article it belongs to. 
• Stay fresh every day. Check the sitemap daily. Find pages that were added, updated or removed, and update only what changed. A removed article must stop appearing in answers. 
• Answer questions in a chatbot. Give a correct answer, show which articles it came from, and say so honestly when it does not know. 
• Offer a clean API. Tasks 2 and 3 will call your Knowledge Brain over its API. Another developer must be able to use it from your documentation alone. 
• When the chatbot cannot answer, it creates a task for a human expert, and the expert's answer flows back into the memory. 
Task 2 — The Inbox Agent: replies that get better with every email sent 
Build an agent that reads every customer email, finds the facts from the Knowledge Brain, and drafts a reply a human can send in one click. Every time a human edits a draft or writes their own reply, the agent learns, until it can safely answer some kinds of email on its own. 
Language: TypeScript 
Customers write in about everything: a how-to question, a new project request, a refund, a complaint, a quote, a follow-up on old work. A fast, correct reply wins the client, and a slow or wrong one loses them. Today a person reads each email, looks up the answer, writes the reply, and remembers who needs a follow-up. 
Your Inbox Agent works like a smart assistant sitting beside that person. It understands what each email wants and gets the facts by calling the Knowledge Brain's API from Task 1. It then writes a draft in the company's voice. The human sends it, edits it, or writes their own. You decide what the agent learns from each of these, how it stores what it learned, and how you prove it is getting better and not worse.
Once humans have sent 20 drafts of the same kind in a row without changing them, that kind of email may go out on its own. You decide what "the same kind" and "without changing" mean. You also decide how the agent loses that freedom when quality drops. 
There is no data pack. Write at least 50 realistic customer emails yourself, covering every kind of request you can think of. We will also test your agent with emails you have never seen. 
Task 3 — Meeting to Scope of Work: one hour, not one week 
Turn messy client meeting transcripts into a scope of work, a developer estimate and a proposal the client can say yes to. When the next meeting changes things, update the scope instead of starting over. 
Language: Python 
After a client meeting, someone writes the scope of work, developers estimate the hours, a senior checks it, and then the proposal goes out. Each step takes days. Clients speak loosely ("make the search better"), while developers need exact tasks. Items get missed, the scope grows quietly, and the company loses money on work it never quoted. 
Design a team of AI agents for this. You choose the roles and decide what each one hands to the next. The team reads one or more transcripts and asks the questions a senior consultant would ask. It produces three things: a scope of work, a task list with hour estimates, and a client-ready proposal. Many requests can be solved with a setting the product already has, which costs the client far less than custom code. Your agents must check this by calling the Knowledge Brain from Task 1. 
Then a second meeting changes some items. Show what changed, what is new, and what falls outside the agreed scope and should be quoted extra. Transcripts are long, so handle that well. Nothing reaches the client until a human approves it. 
There is no data pack. Role-play at least 3 client meetings with a friend, record them, and use the transcripts. One of them must be a follow-up meeting that changes the first.
Rules for students 
Each student builds above capstone alone in 4 days and submits one repo, a short write-up and one demo video. These rules apply to everyone. 
Time: 4 days from the moment you open this brief. 
Language: use only the language named for each task. Python means modern Python: async, type hints, FastAPI or similar. TypeScript means strict mode, typed end to end, Node.js or Next.js. Do not mix languages inside one task. 
IN CASE STUDENTS DON'T NOW TYPESCRIPT, THEY CAN USE PYTHON, BUT DON'T MIX. 
STUDENTS KNOWING BOTH LANGUAGE, GETS 10% EXCESS SCORES IN FINAL SELECTION. 
Models: Claude must be at the center. You may add other models where they help, but you must prove each choice with numbers. Even within Claude which model you will use, you need to justify your decision with 20-30 evals for each such decisions. 
AI tools: Claude Code or Cursor or Antigravity or Codex, are allowed and expected. Use any one tool only and we will need to see your all chat conversations end to end, one or more chat threads. 
We judge your thinking, not your typing.
